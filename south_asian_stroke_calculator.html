Stroke and ICH Risk Tool for South Asians

A research prototype aimed at serving adults of the South Asian diaspora, adults of South Asian ancestry residing in the US, UK, or Canada.
Disclaimer: This is a research prototype, not a validated clinical instrument. This tool is derived from published epidemiological studies rather than prospective clinical validation, and has not undergone formal regulatory review. It is intended for investigative and educational use and should not be used to guide clinical decision-making.

Standard cardiovascular risk equations, such as the Pooled Cohort Equations (PCE) used in current ACC/AHA guidelines, are derived predominantly from White and Black American cohorts. Multiple South Asian cohort studies have found that these equations underestimate observed 10-year event rates in this population (Pursnani et al. 2022 ↗; Patel et al. 2021 ↗).

South Asians have also been shown to present with earlier-onset atherosclerotic disease at lower BMI and cholesterol thresholds than Western reference populations (Volgman et al. 2018 ↗).

This tool applies a South Asian-specific recalibration to the standard formula and, separately, a stroke-subtype-specific relative-risk model derived from INTERSTROKE and Canadian population cohort data.

From this tool, two outputs are produced: a general cardiovascular risk estimate and a stroke-subtype-stratified estimate (ischemic vs. hemorrhagic). A summarized methodology is available under each result.

Why this matters: this tool demonstrates how a risk estimate changes when South Asian-specific data replace a default reference population. Each result below shows the standard estimate next to the recalibrated one, so the difference is visible directly rather than reported only in the paper. It is built to illustrate that gap, not to guide an individual treatment decision.

Methodology
Baseline estimate uses the standard ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations (PCE), the 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk calculator incorporated into current US guidelines. The PCE derivation cohorts (ARIC, CHS, CARDIA, Framingham Offspring) did not include South Asian participants, and no South Asian-specific coefficient set exists in the original equations.
Two published predicted-vs-observed comparisons in South Asian cohorts inform the recalibration curve applied here. Pursnani et al. 2022 (Kaiser Permanente) found a PCE-predicted risk of 1.8% against an observed risk of 4.9% in a low-predicted-risk stratum. Patel et al. 2021 (UK Biobank) found a predicted risk of 4.8% against an observed risk of 6.8%. These two anchor points define a log-log calibration curve, with the multiplier decreasing as PCE-predicted risk increases. A country-of-origin multiplier (Patel et al. 2021, UK Biobank subgroup hazard ratios) is applied when a specific country of origin is selected.
The recalibration figure above should be interpreted as one estimate consistent with part of the available evidence, not a settled correction factor.
Risk category (Low / Borderline / Intermediate / High) uses the standard ACC/AHA PCE thresholds (<5%, 5–<7.5%, 7.5–<20%, ≥20%), applied to the recalibrated figure.

Methodology
The estimate combines three components:
  1. A baseline incidence rate for South Asian adults (39.7 ischemic and 5.0 ICH strokes per 100,000 person-years; Khan et al. 2017 ↗, Ontario/British Columbia cohort, age- and sex-standardized).
  2. An age adjustment reflecting how stroke risk changes with age in South Asian immigrants specifically, rather than a generic aging curve (Vyas et al. 2021 ↗).
  3. A relative-risk multiplier built from the risk factors selected above. Each factor carries a South Asia-region odds ratio from the INTERSTROKE ↗ case-control study, calculated separately for ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke (for example, hypertension carries an odds ratio of 3.61 for ischemic stroke and 4.37 for ICH). Selected risk factors are multiplied together to form a combined relative-risk estimate.
Note: multiplying odds ratios assumes each risk factor acts independently. With three or more risk factors present, that assumption is less reliable and tends to overstate true combined risk. A flag appears below the affected estimate when this threshold is met; interpret it as an upper-bound estimate.
Note: family history of stroke is an established risk factor, but no South Asian-specific effect estimate was identified in the literature reviewed; it is not included as a model input.
Risk category (Low / Moderate / Elevated) for ischemic stroke and ICH uses descriptive bands (<1%, 1–<3%, ≥3%) chosen to make the output legible. Unlike Model A's PCE bands, no validated clinical risk tier exists for South Asian stroke-subtype risk; treat these labels as interpretive, not diagnostic.
Derived from published cohort and case-control data (MASALA, UK Biobank, INTERSTROKE, INTERHEART, SABRE, and Canadian population-based studies by Khan et al. and Vyas et al.). No individual patient-level data was used in model construction or testing. Not reviewed or approved for clinical use.
